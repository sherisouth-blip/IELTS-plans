A court for the whole world

Aim: Explain what the International Criminal Court (ICC) is and why Duterte is on trial there, using the key court words.
Compelling question: Why were most of the victims poor?
Let's get started

Who can judge a president?

A president breaks the law. Who should decide if he is guilty? Choose one and explain why.

the courts in his own country the voters, at the next election a court for the whole world nobody — a president is above the law
Example: I think the voters should decide, because the president works for them.
Let's check

Usually, a country's own courts judge its leaders. But sometimes a country cannot or will not do it. Then a court for the whole world can step in: the International Criminal Court.

Duterte and the ICC

  • 2016–2022 — Rodrigo Duterte is president of the Philippines. Thousands of people are killed in his drug war.
  • 2019 — The Philippines leaves the ICC.
  • March 2025 — Duterte is arrested in Manila and flown to The Hague.
  • April 2026 — The ICC says it can still judge him, because the crimes happened while the Philippines was a member. It confirms three charges of crimes against humanity.
  • 30 November 2026 — The trial begins.
Important: Duterte is accused, not convicted. Like everyone at the ICC, he is presumed innocent. The prosecutor must prove each charge with evidence, the defence will answer, and the judges will decide.
The three charges (what the prosecutor says happened; the trial will decide)
  1. Killings in the city of Davao, when Duterte was mayor (2013–2016)
  2. Killings of "high-value targets" during his presidency
  3. Killings during police operations in barangays (neighbourhoods)
Think about it
  1. Duterte's lawyers said the ICC could not judge him, because the Philippines left in 2019. Do you think that is a fair argument?
  2. Charge 3 is about killings in neighbourhoods. Which neighbourhoods do you think? Connect your answer to the compelling question.
