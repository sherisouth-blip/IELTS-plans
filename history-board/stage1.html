Jay and me

Aim: Compare your life with Jay's, explain what it means to be an insider and an outsider, and notice how a label can replace a person's name.
Compelling question: How can we know what really happened in the past?
Let's get started

A small word in the book

Open your book to the dedication, the short message at the start where the author says who the book is for. Read it aloud.

Randy Ribay dedicated the book to "the hyphenated." What do you think hyphenated means here? Guess first.

people who are very busypeople who belong to two culturespeople who write a lot
Let's check

A hyphen is this small line: -. It joins two words, like Filipino-American or Japanese-Canadian. "The hyphenated" are people who belong to two cultures. The hyphen joins the two words, but they are still two words. Jay is one of these people.

Let's talk

Insider or outsider?

Jay is Filipino-American. In America, people see him as Filipino. In the Philippines, his family sees him as American.

  1. Where is Jay an insider? Where is he an outsider?
  2. Can a person be both at the same time? How?
  3. Have you ever felt like an insider and an outsider at the same time? (You choose what to share.)
Example: I feel like an insider with my friends, but an outsider when everyone talks about a TV show I have never seen.
Let's think

Groups and labels

Think about your school (now or before).

  1. What groups are there? Make a list. For example: the sports team, the students who always study, the gamers…
  2. Are you in one of these groups? Which one, if any?
  3. What do people call each group? Write the name people use.
  4. Choose a group you are not in. What are they like? Write three words.
Now think about it

How do you know what that group is like? Have you talked to everyone in it?

When we generalize about a group we do not know, we assume. The group gets a label, and we stop seeing the people inside it. This is how prejudice can begin.

Sort your labels

Which labels only describe a group (like "the basketball team")? Which ones judge a group (like "losers")? Which would hurt if someone used it about you?
